## Paradigms of International Relations

### 100 points

**PAR-100-1**  (Week 1, Waltz; slide: The System Has Three Big Features)

Clue: In Waltz's account, what word describes an international system with no global police force and no authority above states?

Answer: **Anarchy**

- A. Anarchy  ✓
- B. Hierarchy
- C. Hegemony
- D. Polarity

Why: Anarchy does not mean chaos. It means there is no overarching authority above states.

**PAR-100-2**  (Week 2, Nye & Welch; slide: Power)

Clue: What do we call the ability to get others to want what you want, through persuasion and not coercion?

Answer: **Soft power**

- A. Soft power  ✓
- B. Hard power
- C. Relative power
- D. Fungibility

Why: Hard power gets another actor to do what it otherwise would not; soft power gets others to want what you want.

**PAR-100-3**  (Week 1, Nye; slide: Realism)

Clue: Which paradigm holds that states are the dominant actors, power is the key difference between them, and competition is their primary interaction?

Answer: **Realism**

- A. Realism  ✓
- B. Liberalism
- C. Constructivism
- D. Marxism

Why: For realists, all of international politics sits in the shadow of potential conflict, and even cooperation reflects the balance of power.

**PAR-100-4**  (Week 2, Nye & Welch (and Week 11); slide: IR System Power Configurations)

Answer: **Bipolar**

- A. Bipolar  ✓
- B. Unipolar
- C. Multipolar
- D. Hegemonic

Why: Unipolar means one dominant power, bipolar two, and multipolar three or more.

## Final Jeopardy

**FIN-1**  (Week 10, Feaver & Lorber; Johns; slide: The only sanctions we see applied are cases where the threat of sanctions did not work.)

- B. A coordination problem
